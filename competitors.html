ONDA Menu Competitors
Positioning and market scan · updated October 24, 2026 · for David Richardson

Explore a place. Make it a home.

ONDA Menu is a personal travel atlas. Your places, your restaurants and their menus sit on one map in layers, anchored by the homebases you stayed at, and the map keeps growing with every trip.

Most travel apps help you organize where you're going. ONDA Menu also helps you remember where you've been and why it mattered. This page explains the layered map first, then a day and a lifetime of using it, then each competitor in detail.

Three layers, one map

The map is the organizing idea. Each layer switches on and off by itself, so the same map answers different questions: where am I going, where can I eat, what have others found.

📍🏠 My Places · only on your phone

Where you go, and where you stay

Landmarks, galleries, markets, interesting streets, pins with a note and a date, and every homebase you've had. Find your way back.

🍴 My Restaurants · only on your phone

Where you eat

Restaurants with their menus read and translated, dishes you want to try, meals you rated, your notes and photos. Eat there again.

● ONDA Guide · shared

What others have found

Restaurants and menus added by other diners, with no personal data. Places with dishes that suit you turn teal. Save one, and it moves into My Restaurants with its menu.

Every place also hands its exact spot to Google Maps for walking or driving directions. Pins can be moved to the right door by long-pressing them; the change stays on your phone.

The living travel atlas

A homebase is more than a place to navigate back to. It anchors a whole collection of experiences. Pinch out, drag to Chiang Mai, and your homebase is there with its date, surrounded by the places you pinned and the restaurants you ate at. You don't file your travels; you find them where they happened.

A day in Chiang Mai, step by step

Three weeks in a city you don't know, a language you don't read. This is the order the day happens in, and what ONDA Menu does at each step.

  1. The night before · hotel

    Plan the day

    On a laptop, look around the neighborhoods: places to eat, a temple, a market, a viewpoint. Read menus before you go.

    ONDA Menu map, Google Maps in another tab

  2. The night before

    Build your own map

    Save the restaurants, drop pins with a note, set the hotel as your homebase. Everything sits in layers you can switch on and off.

    My Places · My Restaurants · ONDA Guide

  3. Before you leave

    Carry it with you

    Send the plan to your phone by AirDrop or email. No account, no sign-in. Restaurants arrive with their menus already read.

    A small places file, about 250 bytes a place

  4. Midday · in line

    Understand and order

    Photograph the menu or open the one you saved. See the dishes in your language, filtered to how you eat, then tap one to show the cook.

    Translated · ✓ printed or "likely" · show the cook

  5. All day

    Get there, and get back

    Each saved place hands its exact spot to Google Maps for walking or driving. At the end of the day, your homebase is one tap away.

    Directions · homebase

  6. That evening, and years later

    Remember

    Mark what you had, rate it, add a note. The map fills in as the trip goes, and it stays on your phone.

    I had this · stars · notes · pins with dates

The day stays enjoyable because the uncertainty is handled in advance: you know where you're going, what you can eat there, and how to get home.

Who it's for

Starting customer

The independent, curious, long-stay traveler

Wants to get to know a place over weeks rather than follow someone else's itinerary. Values discovery, personal freedom and keeping the experience. Enjoys local food without speaking the language. Not necessarily a frequent traveler; someone who wants familiarity with a place, not just its attractions.

Also served

People with diets or restrictions

Find dishes that fit instead of judging restaurants blindly. Printed labels are marked ✓; guesses are marked "likely". For allergies, always ask the staff.

Also served

Curious local diners

Look at nearby menus before going, save dishes to try, remember what they liked. The Oak Hill and Triangle pilot in Austin starts here.

Pays later

Independent restaurant owners

Verified owners correct their menu, place their pin and show real dish photos. A paying customer, not the person the app is designed around.

This is a proposed customer profile drawn from the product and this scan, not yet confirmed by people using the app repeatedly. Friends testing through TestFlight are the first check.

Where ONDA Menu stands

Crowded

Reading and translating a menu is no longer special

Google Lens does it free, ChatGPT and Gemini do it well, and a dozen small apps sell it by subscription. ONDA Menu shouldn't lead with "translate".

Gap you spotted

Most rivals show dish photos; ONDA Menu doesn't

Yelp's Menu Vision, Google Maps' popular dishes and most translator apps put a picture next to the dish. Yelp cites a YouGov survey: 4 in 5 diners say dish photos help them order.

Different

Nobody joins "read the menu" with "remember what I had"

Translators forget the menu once you close them. Dish diaries make you type every dish by hand. ONDA Menu turns the menu into the diary: tap "I had this" on the real list.

Different

Honest labels, private by default

"Printed on the menu" vs "likely" is rare; most scanners say Safe/Avoid as if certain. No account, notes on the phone, and a shared menu guide that holds no personal data.

Different

A local menu guide, built by neighbors

browse.onda.menu plus the monthly drawing is a community play none of the apps make. Google and Yelp have the data but not the neighborhood focus.

Different

Explore ranks by dishes that suit you, not by fame

Google's "Your match" and Yelp's diet preferences score whole restaurants, mostly from reviews. On ONDA Menu's map, places with dishes that pass your filter turn teal; tap one for those dishes, then save it with its menu, ready to mark and rate. Diet maps like HappyCow and Find Me Gluten Free cover one diet, place by place. The weak spot is coverage: a place ranks only once someone has added its menu.

Different

A personal travel atlas that stays yours

Google My Maps, Wanderlog and Mapstr organize places in your account, built for trips and sharing. ONDA Menu keeps My Places and My Restaurants on the phone as layers, anchored by homebases over the years, with the menus read, and hands directions to Google Maps.

Watch

Google Maps is moving fastest

Ask Maps (Gemini) now finds dishes from photos, reviews and menus, and since August 2026 can order takeout. Google could add "remember what you ordered" any time.

The dish-photo question

You're right that people want to see a dish before they order it — it's the one place most competitors are ahead. Four ways ONDA Menu could add photos, from cheapest to richest:

Quick

"See photos" link per dish

Tap a dish to open an image search for its name (and the restaurant's) in the browser.

Free, no rights issues, a day of work. Photos aren't from this restaurant.

Recommended first

Owners add their own photos

Part of the owner subscription: a verified restaurant uploads real photos of its dishes, shown to everyone. Gives owners a concrete reason to pay.

Fits the planned $29.99–$39.99/year plan. Needs photo storage and a simple upload screen.

Later

Diners share their food photos

Let people choose to share the photo they took of a dish (today these stay on the phone).

Most like Yelp. Brings moderation work, Apple's user-content rules (report and block tools) and a privacy-policy change.

Careful

"Typical dish" illustrations

A generic picture per dish type (pho, enchiladas), clearly labeled "typical — not this restaurant's".

Cheap once made and reused. Risk of disappointing people if their plate looks different.

A diner subscription for photos is possible, but rivals give dish photos away free (Yelp, Google), so charging diners for them would be hard. Charging owners for showing their own dishes is the stronger fit.

The competitors, one by one

Google Lens and Translate

Built into Android and the Google app

Does
Point the camera at a menu, see it translated over the page.
Strength
Free, everywhere, works offline with downloaded languages.
Gap
Translates words, not dishes: no explanation, no allergens, nothing saved.

Google Maps (Ask Maps, popular dishes)

Google · Gemini inside Maps

Does
Popular dishes with user photos; Lens scans uploaded menu photos and stars popular items; Ask Maps finds a dish nearby and, since August 2026, orders takeout through Square and Toast.
Strength
Huge photo and review base; owners already edit menus free.
Gap
No personal dish diary, no printed-vs-likely labels, nothing to show the cook. Saved places live in your Google account, not on your phone.
Risk
Highest. Could add "remember what you ordered" any time.

Yelp Menu Vision

Yelp · launched October 2025, upgraded spring 2026

Does
Point the camera at a menu; photos and reviews of each dish pop up over it, with "Popular" badges.
Strength
Real photos from that restaurant.
Gap
English menus only; needs 5+ dish photos already on Yelp; no translation, no diary.

Menu translator apps

MenuGuide, Menu Pixie, What to Eat, Picture Menu, MenuVista and others

Does
Snap a menu; get translation, dish photos, some allergens and currency.
Strength
Dish photos and a travel focus.
Gap
Mostly paid subscriptions (MenuGuide listed at $4.99 a week); nothing remembered after the meal; photo sources often unclear.

Dish diaries

Savor, My Food Rating, Palytt, Crumble, FoodFavs

Does
Rate the dishes you eat, keep photos, search your food history; some share ratings with friends.
Strength
Same "remember what I loved" need as ONDA Menu.
Gap
You type every dish yourself; none read the menu for you. Mostly small apps.

Beli

Ranking app · 120M+ ratings in 50,000 cities (Boston Globe, Aug 2026)

Does
Rank restaurants against each other; social lists of where friends went.
Strength
Big, fast-growing, loved by younger diners.
Gap
Restaurants, not dishes; social, not private; no menus.

Allergy menu scanners

Alleri, Food Allergy Options, Allergy Lens, FoodPickAI, SafeEat

Does
Enter your allergies; scan a menu; each dish marked Safe, Caution or Avoid.
Strength
Sharp focus on one worry.
Gap
Sounds more certain than a photo allows; no memory of what was safe last time.

ChatGPT and Gemini

General AI assistants with cameras

Does
Read, translate and explain any menu; answer "what's vegetarian here?".
Strength
People already have them; very capable.
Gap
A chat, not a record: no dish list, no ratings, no map, nothing shared with neighbors. About 1 in 5 UK diners already use AI to plan a visit (NIQ, Oct 2026).

What this suggests for ONDA Menu

  • Lead with the atlas. "Explore a place. Make it a home." Three layers on one map, anchored by homebases, growing with every trip. Translation and menus are how the food part works.
  • Build sharing next. Sending a trip's map as a places file turns the atlas into something people show friends, and it's the one atlas promise not built yet.
  • Start with the long-stay traveler. People who stay weeks and want to know a place have the clearest need for the layers and the homebases. Local diners and owners follow.
  • Tell the counter story. Photo of the menu, a short list of dishes that suit you, then the dish shown large for the cook. Ninety seconds from line to order is easy to show in a demo and hard for a chat assistant to match.
  • Make the map the trip. Restaurants, pins and homebase as layers you own turn ONDA Menu into a private travel memory, and Directions keeps Google Maps doing what it does best.
  • Own the honesty. "✓ printed on the menu" vs "likely" is a real difference in a field that overpromises on allergies.
  • Make the local guide the moat. Big apps have more data but no reason for Oak Hill neighbors to add menus; the drawing and browse.onda.menu do.
  • Close the photo gap through owners. Real dish photos as part of the owner subscription gives owners a reason to pay and diners what they ask for.
  • Watch Google Maps. Check each quarter what Ask Maps does with menus and saved places; that's the competitor that could make a feature disappear overnight.
